## ENTRY
**Category:** Run your mystery through this
**Name:** Fair-play audit
**Description:** Confirm every clue the detective uses in the reveal was actually shown to the reader beforehand.
**Items:**
  - List every fact the detective cites in the final reveal, one line each
  - For each fact, find the earlier page where it was shown to the reader
  - If a fact appears for the first time in the reveal, either plant it earlier in the draft or cut it from the solution
  - Check that the culprit had a genuine, demonstrated opportunity to commit the crime as described
  - Check the detective's method is consistent with how they solved earlier cases in the book

## ENTRY
**Category:** Run your suspects through this
**Name:** Suspect viability checklist
**Description:** Every named suspect should hold up as a plausible culprit until the moment they're cleared.
**Items:**
  - Does this suspect have a real, independently sufficient motive?
  - Does this suspect have a genuine opportunity, not just proximity?
  - Is there at least one piece of true evidence that looks bad for them?
  - Is their eventual innocence explained by something other than the detective simply asserting it?
  - Could a careful reader have suspected this person seriously, not just in passing?

## ENTRY
**Category:** Run your misdirection through this
**Name:** Red-herring checklist
**Description:** Confirm your false leads are honest — built from true, selectively emphasized facts, not fabricated evidence.
**Items:**
  - Is every fact supporting this red herring actually true within the story, not a lie the narrative later has to retract?
  - Does the false suspect have real motive and opportunity, not just a single suspicious detail?
  - Would this red herring survive a reader rereading the book knowing the real answer?
  - Is the misdirection built from emphasis and reader assumption, rather than withheld information?
  - Does clearing this suspect reveal something genuinely useful about the case, rather than being a dead end?

## ENTRY
**Category:** Run your draft through this
**Name:** Clue-plant checklist
**Description:** Confirm your clues are distributed and disguised well enough to survive a careful reader without being invisible.
**Items:**
  - Is each key clue attached to a scene that has another reason to exist, rather than existing only to deliver the clue?
  - Are your clues spaced across the manuscript rather than clustered in one section or the final act?
  - Could a first-time reader plausibly notice this clue without already knowing the solution?
  - Does the clue read naturally on a first pass, and only become obviously significant on a reread?
  - Have you mapped every clue against the chapter it appears in, to check the overall distribution?

## ENTRY
**Category:** Run your detective through this
**Name:** Detective-method-consistency checklist
**Description:** Confirm your detective's investigative method stays consistent across the book, so it reads as character rather than convenience.
**Items:**
  - What kind of evidence does this detective trust, and what kind do they distrust? Is that consistent across every case?
  - What question does this detective always ask first when arriving at a scene?
  - Does every major deduction show its reasoning chain, even if compressed, rather than asserting a conclusion?
  - Does the detective ever get something wrong in a way consistent with their established blind spots?
  - Would a reader who knows this detective's method be able to predict, roughly, how they'd approach a new case?

## ENTRY
**Category:** Run your ending through this
**Name:** Denouement / reveal-scene checklist
**Description:** Confirm the final reveal scene performs the solution rather than just reciting it.
**Items:**
  - Does the detective walk through more than one suspect's possible guilt before naming the true culprit?
  - Is the mechanism (the "how") revealed before the culprit's identity (the "who"), where the structure allows it?
  - Does each piece of evidence get reintroduced in the reveal in a way that reconnects it to where it first appeared?
  - Is there at least one moment in the reveal that reframes a scene the reader thought they already understood?
  - Does the scene leave any loose thread deliberately open, or are all threads accounted for?
